## What they want
- Restaurants, hotels, caterers and professional kitchens buy through **AI agents**. The aim is to make purchasing faster and easier for chefs and purchasing managers.
- **Preferred shape:** an AI shopping assistant **integrated into the existing website**, not a separate app and **not SMS**,
  because the catalogue is too big for that.
- **Must support:** customer-specific pricing, real-time availability and a large catalogue.
- **Inputs:** a description of the need, an **uploaded photo**, or **catering requirements**. The AI then builds the order.
- **Business value:** a competitive edge, and winning customers from competitors through a better buying experience.
- **Future:** move toward autonomous purchasing while keeping the human experience simple.

## Answers from Valio on Discord (Tommi, Sat 14 Nov 09:35 and 09:40)
Tommi's answers, condensed. They reply to questions from several teams, so they apply to everyone.

**How approval works today** (another team asked what needs sign-off, and who signs off)
- Valio's systems have **no approval gates** for orders today and enforce nothing.
- If a customer has spending or decision limits for employees, the customer handles that with **internal instructions**, or by
  letting **only the manager place orders**.
- **In an agentic scenario a human approval would be required at this point.** That could maybe be dropped in the future if
  reliability is good enough.

**Mock API and webshop**
- A webshop account is available at the booth, but it is production, so orders can't be placed. The challenge has **no order
  API**: it is enough that the solution produces **orders for human approval**; they don't need to be sent anywhere.
- `closingTime` is the cut-off for the related `availableDate`. The mock data stays the same all weekend (in real life it shifts).
- Availability **≥ 999999 = unknown**; current logic allows ordering **any quantity** (typical for Terminal products Valio
  doesn't stock). **Empty = not available.**
- `allowedLotSize` 0.01 must be treated as **1** (no fractional crates).
- Rate limit: hitting it returns **429 and a 30-second block**. Honour 429. A few hundred calls per minute is OK; that many in
  10 seconds is not.

**What this means for us**
- Our mandate is **new value, not a copy of an existing process**: today the limits live in customers' internal instructions.
  The gateway turns those instructions into rules enforced on every order, whoever (or whatever agent) sends it.
- **A human approval stays in the loop for agent orders.** That matches our flow: the buyer confirms every order, and the
  mandate decides whether a second human (the manager) is needed. Fully autonomous ordering is a roadmap setting, not the
  demo default.
- Who signs off is customer-specific (the chef, or only the manager places orders): make the approver a per-customer setting.
- Our in-memory order record is acceptable: nothing needs to be sent to Valio.
- The "Tension with the handout" point about outside agents is unchanged.
